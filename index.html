    Breadth-First Search

    BFS keeps a queue of boards and always expands the oldest one first, so it checks every board that is one move away, then two moves away, and so on.

    The first time it reaches the goal, the route is the shortest possible. The cost is memory and time: it has no sense of direction and can explore tens of thousands of boards.

    A* Search

    A* ranks each board by moves made so far plus the Manhattan distance, the total number of rows and columns every tile is away from its home spot.

    Because that estimate never overshoots, A* still finds a shortest solution, but it follows the most promising boards first and usually explores far fewer.

    Solvable boards

    Half of all arrangements can never reach the goal. The solver counts inversions, pairs of tiles that are out of order ignoring the blank, and only runs when that count is even.
